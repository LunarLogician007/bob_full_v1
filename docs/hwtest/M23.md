# M23 hardware test: 10 × 10 CLBs (crossbar OR roots, routing muxes from primitives)

**This milestone needs a Vivado rebuild.** `hw/build.cfg`:
- `tag = M23`
- `top = bob_top`
- `idcode = 0B023093`
- `usercode = 00000017` (23)
- `delays = 1`: the crossbar (leaves + an OR) and every routing mux (a LUT6/MUXF7/MUXF8 tree)
  change their delays, so time them again. This is also the first build with the fixed
  delay extraction (M22 measured nothing).

## What changed

The sweep (`software/bob/gridsweep.py`, `docs/reports/M23/grid_sweep.md`) showed that
M22's wall was **SLICEM slices**, not LUTs. The CFGLUT5s of one SLICEM share a shift enable,
so they can only come from one CLB, and Vivado packed 3.37 of them per slice: 84% of the
SLICEMs at 81 CLBs. Two changes:

- **Crossbar OR roots** (`hw/src/clb/lxor.v`). A crossbar mux keeps its five CFGLUT5
  leaves, but an unselected leaf holds zeros, so the root needs no table: it is a fixed OR
  in a plain LUT. This cuts **152 → 128 CFGLUT5 per CLB**. The bitstream is unchanged; the
  loader expands the same 5-bit selects.
- **Routing muxes from primitives** (`hw/src/fabric/bob_mux.v`): LUT6 4:1 leaves on
  sel[1:0], MUXF7 on sel[2], MUXF8 on sel[3] (a 16:1 mux in one slice). Vivado made
  ~18.5k LUTs of M22's routing; the same muxes as primitives are ~12.9k.

| | M22 | M23 |
|---|---|---|
| CLBs | 81 (9 × 9) | **100 (10 × 10)** |
| LUTs | 324 | **400** |
| configuration | 56,448 bits, 441 frames | 68,096 bits, 532 frames |
| routing | W = 40, fc_in 0.15 | W = 36, fc_in 0.10 (fir16 needs 34, every other example 20) |
| pads | 40 | 44 |
| BRAM / DSP | 2 / 2 | 2 / 2 (columns 5 rows tall) |

**The first M23 build (12 × 11, 2026-09-24) did not place.** It shared each crossbar leaf
between two muxes through the CFGLUT5's O5 and O6 outputs. Vivado maps a dual-output CFGLUT5
to SRL16E + SRLC32E, two LUT sites. The placer reported "Weighted LUTRAM utilization is more
than 100" and hung in "Commit Most Macros & LUTRAMs". Every leaf now uses O6 only (one
SRLC32E, as at M22).

Two limits of the old 16-bit world are fixed on the way (the chain is past 65,536 bits):
- `cfg_ctrl.v` counts the chain in 24 bits. The 16-bit counter saturated, so a 68k-bit
  chain would never have committed. CFG_CTRL still reports the low 16 bits; `len_err` is
  the full comparison.
- The L-bit mask is a frame → kind table (`BOB_LKIND`/`BOB_LMASKS`), because iverilog's
  widest constant is 65,536 bits.

## Size before the build

`software/bob/synth_estimate.sh` (yosys, whole design):

| | logic LUTs | CFGLUT5 | FFs | yosys peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M22 (Vivado: 38,184 LUT / 25,599 FF, 82% slices) | 36,980 | 12,312 | 25,449 | 5.1 GB |
| **M23** | **25,691** | **12,800** | **26,787** | 1.9 GB |

Projected in Vivado: **33k–37k LUTs (62–70%)** and 66–76% of the slices. SLICEM LUTs are
at 73.6%. SLICEM slices are 87% at M22's packing (3.37 per slice) and 74% if Vivado packs
four.

- `runme.log` after "Phase 1.3 Build Placer Netlist Model" must **not** show
  `Weighted LUTRAM utilization is more than 100`. If it does, stop the run and send
  `runme.log`. The fallback is 10 × 9 (`ARCH_M23` `ny` 10 → 9 in `software/bob/device.py`,
  then `make rrgraph device vpr`).
- "Phase 3.2 Commit Most Macros & LUTRAMs" took about 1.5 minutes at M22. If it runs
  past 20 minutes, stop the run and send `runme.log`.
- The two `place_report:` lines in `runme.log` must both show a positive WNS.

## Build (Windows)

1. Replace `E:\bob_full_v1\hw` with this `hw/` (from the `bob_full_v1_m23` worktree) and
   run `build.tcl`.
2. Program the board. Copy the **whole** `bob_vivado\out\M23\` folder to
   `docs/reports/M23/`: timing, utilization, DRC, `sysclk_1cycle.txt`, `delay_paths.rpt`.
3. On the Mac, in the M23 checkout:
   ```sh
   software/bob/delays.py fold docs/reports/M23/delay_paths.rpt
   make check
   ```

## Automatic (`make hwtest M=M23`), about 40 minutes

The full M22 list runs first on the bigger fabric: every configuration path, partial
reconfiguration, the BRAM frames, the block designs, the waveform, clock and cluster
checks, and lutram-snake (now 400 elements). Then the new check:

| check | pass means |
|---|---|
| xbar-pins | The snake again, but element k reads the chain on pin `snake_pin(k)`, its other pins are const1, and the element is the AND of them all. Every crossbar mux of every CLB then carries the chain somewhere on the chip, and every other mux must deliver const1. It loads over frames, then its complement over the chain. LD0 must be SW0 xor the mask's parity for both switch positions both times. The M22 snake only used pin 0, so it could miss a dead crossbar input. |

## By hand (`--manual`), about 10 minutes

- [ ] live-fir16: put SW0 up and hold **BTN0**. The LEDs settle to a steady pattern in about two seconds. Release BTN0: the LEDs hold.
- [ ] Hold **BTN1**: every LED goes off.
- [ ] In the studio (`./bob studio --probe usb`), open `work/examples/fir16/fir16.v` and **Generate Bitstream**. The Device view shows the 10 × 10 grid of CLBs, each with its four element cells.
- [ ] `./bob build work/examples/big/big.v --clock run --hz auto -o build/bit/big.bit`: the timing line shows the critical path.
