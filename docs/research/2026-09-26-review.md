# bob, reviewed end to end (2026-09-26)

A review of the whole repository at `main` (M25 on the board, hardware locked) against the
references the project uses: AMD UG470/UG474/UG953, OpenFPGA and SOFA, FABulous, Aegis
(`/Users/sk/work/aegis`), F4PGA/prjxray, Intel's LAB/ALM, iCE40 (IceStorm), VTR/VPR and the
papers cited below. The aim it is measured against: **an FPGA built from scratch**. Today
that means RTL running inside the PYNQ-Z2; physical design is the next phase.

Nothing here was run on the board. Every finding marked **verified** was checked in the
code, in `software/bob/device.json`, in the committed reports or in UG470's text; the
counts come from short scripts over `device.json` (the same ones `docs/learn/layers/gen.py`
now runs for chapter 13). The ideas already in `docs/research/2026-09-24-fpga-ideas.md`
(the ARM configuration port, LUT networks, BRAM→DSP directs, bitstream screening) are not
repeated; they still stand.

## 0. The short version

bob is unusually complete for its size: two configuration protocols on one memory,
UG470-shaped packets with CRC, partial reconfiguration that keeps state, time travel, a
fabric generated from VPR's own routing graph, two place-and-route flows, and a
verification habit (golden co-simulation, mutants, a stand-in board) that most academic
fabric generators do not have. The weak points are not in what was built but in what the
numbers rest on: the router is tuned for someone else's chip, the connection boxes are
thinner than the architecture file says, the timing model is sampled, and the host link
is slower than its TCK. None of those needs the hardware reopened except the first.

## 1. Verified findings

### 1.1 Thin connection boxes, unevenly spread (architecture)

`ARCH_M23` sets `fc_in = 0.10` at W = 36. VPR (`alloc_and_load_actual_fc`, the tileable
builder uses the same function) computes `fac × sets × fc` per port and hands the total out
**two tracks at a time**, round-robin over the port's pins. For the CLB's 16 `I` pins that
is 58 connections: 13 pins get 4 tracks and **3 pins get 2**. Over the device, 364 IPINs
have 2 tracks and 1,856 have 4 (300 of the 2-track pins are CLB inputs; the rest are BRAM
and DSP data pins). The HANDOFF's SERV failure, "4 overused CLB input pins at fc_in 0.10",
is this.

The fix costs almost nothing on this host. `bob_mux.v` builds an IPIN mux from LUT6 leaves
of four entries (const0, const1 and the tracks), so 4, 5 or 6 tracks all fit in **two
leaves and three select bits**: going from 4 to 6 tracks per pin is free, and only the 364
two-track pins (one leaf) gain a LUT and a bit. Use an absolute Fc so every pin gets the
same (`<fc in_type="abs" in_val="6" ...>`), or `fc_in` 0.15 (6 tracks for 11 pins, 4 for 5).

Why the M23 sweep did not show this: at 10 × 10 its fc_in 0.15 row was priced at W = 40,
because fir16's **single-seed** minimum channel width came out at 38 there (34 at 0.10),
which is VPR noise, not a real need. Every other example needs 20 tracks. Before the next
hardware milestone: rerun `gridsweep.py` with 3–5 seeds per point and absolute Fc 4/6 at
W 36, and route SERV on each graph.

### 1.2 VPR is timing-driven on another chip's delays (CAD)

The committed `software/bob/arch/bob_k6.xml` carries the OpenFPGA 40 nm numbers: routing
switch 58 ps, connection box 72 ps, LUT 261 ps, crossbar 95 ps. bob's host numbers
(`delays.json`) are routing mux 4.60 ns, input mux 4.65 ns, crossbar 3.01 ns, LUT ≈3.4 ns.
The ratios differ, not just the scale: in the reference a LUT costs 4.5 routing hops, on bob
less than one. VPR's criticality weights in packing, placement and routing are therefore
tuned for the wrong fabric, and the "critical path" VPR prints for bob's designs means
nothing (GUIDE §5.3 says so).

`sweep.py` already writes bob's delays into an architecture (`arch["delays"]`). Doing the
same for the committed arch changes the rr graph's switch delays but not its topology, so
`bob_fabric.v` should come out byte-identical: check that, then `make rrgraph && make vpr`
and compare `timing.py`'s critical paths per example before and after. Software only; the
board keeps its bitstream.

### 1.3 The timing contract's delays are sampled (CAD / safety)

`delays.json` holds, per class, the maximum over the samples `delays.py plan` picked: 32
routing muxes, 53 input muxes, 91 crossbar muxes, out of 3,475 / 2,220 / 2,400. LUT, carry
and flip-flop delays are still estimates, so the contract runs with the 2× provisional
guard band. A sampled maximum is not a maximum, so the guard band is also covering for the
samples. Vivado can dump every cell and net delay of the routed M25 checkpoint in one pass:
`write_sdf` (the same file timing simulation uses), or `get_net_delays` on the `r<node>`
nets, which survive flattening (PLAN §M20). Parsing that gives a delay **per mux**, not per
class. The contract then sums the real delays of the design's own path, and the guard band
can drop to the measured 1.25×: every guest design gets faster without touching the
hardware. The board's `clock-margin` sweep already exists to prove it.

### 1.4 The host link, not TCK, sets load time (software)

`Probe.shift_dr` sends one USB transaction per bit, and every IR scan, USER1 word and
`CFG_CTRL` access goes through it. `shift_dr_fast` bulk-shifts 480 bits per packet but still
does the TAP walk and the ragged tail pulse by pulse. The board's own `fast-tck` check
measured the result: 13 designs loaded in 28.3 s at 100 kHz and 11.0 s at 1 MHz (2.6×) on
the first run, and 12.6 s at both rates (1.0×) on the second. A 10× faster TCK buys little.

- **Queue TAP operations** (OpenOCD's model): build a whole scan, TMS walk included, as one
  sequence of DirtyJTAG commands, and ask for TDO only where it is read. IR scans, USER1
  and CAPTURE become one or two packets each.
- **Load only the frames that differ from zero.** `packets.load_stream` writes all 532
  frames. After JPROGRAM the memory is zero and the loader has swept every CFGLUT5 to zero,
  so a full load needs only the non-zero runs (`_frame_runs` already exists for partial
  loads). The counter example uses 26 frames of 532 (volume 3). Keep the full FDRO readback
  as the proof.

### 1.5 Clock mode 0 at 1 MHz (RTL semantics, host guard)

In clock mode 0 with USER1 `ce`, `clock_ctrl.v` asks for one user-clock pulse per TCK
rising edge, but pulses are never closer than the gap, and only **one** early request
waits. With `clk_gap` = 0 the gap is 512 sysclk cycles, 4,096 ns; TCK at 1 MHz has an edge
every 1,000 ns. Any bulk shift in mode 0 would merge edges. The `counter-step` checks still
pass because `shift_dr` spends a USB round trip on every edge. Words built by the flow set
their own `clk_gap` from the critical path (tens of ns), so they are fine; hand designs from
`designs.py` and old `.bit` files keep the default. Guard it in software: refuse mode-0
stepping faster than the word's gap (the contract has the numbers), or have `designs.py`
set `clk_gap` like the flow does. `docs/bitstream-format.md` §11 now says this.

### 1.6 bob studio's local API (security)

`software/host/studio.py` serves on 127.0.0.1 (port 8765 in browser mode) with no session
token and no Host or Origin check, and `_body()` parses any request body as JSON. A web page
open in the same browser can therefore POST `text/plain` bodies (no CORS preflight) to
`/api/save` (rewrite any `.v`/`.sv`/`.vh` file inside the repo, `hw/src` included),
`/api/project/new` (create folders anywhere under an existing directory) or `/api/program`.
DNS rebinding would even let it read responses. The standard fix, as Jupyter does: a
random token made at start-up, put into the page and required as a header on every `/api/`
call (a custom header forces a preflight the server never grants), plus a Host check
against `127.0.0.1:<port>` and `localhost:<port>`.

### 1.7 Smaller verified items

- **`make check`'s banner.** `check: device sim lint test`, but `=== make check: all green ===`
  is echoed by the `device` recipe, so it prints before any simulation runs. Put it in a
  `check` recipe.
- **IEEE 1149.1 details for silicon.** TDO is driven low outside Shift-IR/DR (the standard
  wants it inactive, high-impedance, there); no TRST (optional); and the IDCODE ends in
  `0x093`, which is **Xilinx's JEDEC manufacturer code** (0x049). Fine inside a PYNQ, but
  tools that read the chain will take bob for a Xilinx part. A chip of its own needs its own
  JEP106 identity, and a BSDL file (generate it from `device.json`: the boundary order is
  already there).
- **XDC input delays.** `set_input_delay -clock tck` on TMS/TDI refers to the rising edge,
  while the comment (and DirtyJTAG) launch on the falling edge; `-clock_fall` says what
  happens. Harmless at 1 MHz.
- **Readback proves what was written, not what is held** (known, `cfg_store.v`). For
  scrubbing (HANDOFF candidate 2) the CFGLUT5 half can be read for real: shift each CFGLUT5
  32 times with CDO fed back to CDI, which rotates its table out and back. Flip-flop bits
  have no cheap read path on this host; on silicon, bit lines give it for free.
- **INIT is also the SR value** (`ff_rstval`). 7-series keeps INIT (the GSR value) and SRVAL
  (the SR value) separate. One bit per flip-flop more removes yosys's `init = reset`
  legalisation constraint and decouples time travel from the design's reset.

## 2. The architecture, against real FPGAs

What bob has is a faithful, small, VTR-style island FPGA. What follows is where real devices
differ in ways that matter for a chip of its own. Ordered by impact.

### 2.1 Routing

- **One segment type (L4).** Xilinx 7-series mixes single, double, quad-class and long wires
  (prjxray's INT tile); Intel mixes R4/R24-class row and C4/C16-class column wires; iCE40 has
  span-4 and span-12 plus 8 global nets. Betz & Rose (FPGA 1999) and Lemieux et al. (FPT
  2004, unidirectional single-driver wires) are the method. On the emulated fabric a hop is
  a whole host mux (1.7–4.6 ns), so fewer hops on long nets pays more than on silicon. Sweep
  L4 + a fraction of L8/L12 with bob's delays (§1.2) in `sweep.py`.
- **No global or dedicated control networks.** The clock is one global enable, and CE/SR
  are ordinary routed pins. High-fanout enables and resets eat tracks and IPINs (see §1.1).
  Real devices route clocks on dedicated trees and let control sets come from global nets
  (Lamoureux & Wilton, FPGA 2006). OpenFPGA models a programmable clock network
  (`append_clock_rr_graph`, `route_clock_rr_graph`), which fits bob's use of `--read_rr_graph`,
  since VPR's own `--clock_modeling dedicated_network` does not work with a read-in graph.
- **fc** (§1.1).

### 2.2 Logic block

- **Control sets.** One CE and one SR per 4-element CLB, one clock for the fabric. UG474's
  slice has one CE/SR/CLK set per slice; Intel's LAB offers several LAB-wide clocks, enables
  and clears. Flip-flops with different enables cannot share a bob CLB, which wastes elements
  on real designs. Two CE and two SR per CLB is cheap next to the crossbar.
- **No flip-flop bypass.** A flip-flop's D comes only from its LUT or the adder, so a lone
  register costs a LUT (Xilinx has AX–DX, Intel register packing). Double Duty (M24) frees the
  LUT when the adder is used, not otherwise.
- **Asynchronous set/reset.** Rejected in synthesis today. On an enable-clocked fabric an
  "asynchronous" reset can be one that acts on every sysclk edge, not only on gce: within
  8 ns, far inside any guest clock period. Removal still has to be timed, as on any FPGA.
- **Carry chain entry.** A chain that crosses a column break needs a generator element; a
  CYINIT-style select (0, 1, or a routed input, UG474 CARRY4) at each CLB's carry-in removes
  it.

### 2.3 Clocks, I/O, memories

- **Clocks:** one user clock. Multiple gce generators with a per-CLB clock select would let
  designs with two clock domains run (a UART beside a core). On silicon this becomes real
  clock networks and a PLL (Aegis's clock tile wraps a PDK PLL).
- **I/O:** no tristate, no bidirectional pads, no I/O registers, no pull or drive options
  (the host pins have fixed directions). A silicon bob needs all of these in its I/O tile;
  the boundary cells then sit in that tile, as in 1149.1.
- **BRAM:** one aspect ratio (1K × 18) and one write enable per port. RAMB18E1's byte write
  enables are inferable in Vivado's template; the 9-bit mode can use them. Real yosys
  mapping (`memory_libmap`) gets much better with more shapes.
- **DSP:** static OPMODE, add only. Enough for filters; dynamic OPMODE is what makes a DSP a
  small ALU (UG479).

### 2.4 Configuration

- **GCAPTURE.** UG470: capture "stores all CLB and IOB register values into configuration
  memory cells ... the same memory cell that programs the register's initial state", read
  out over FDRO, and "sending the GRESTORE command ... after the Capture sequence can cause
  registers to return" to the captured state. That is Xilinx's native snapshot/restore, and
  what StateMover (Attia & Betz, FPGA 2020) builds on. bob's M25 restore already uses the
  INIT-cell half; a silicon bob should make the INIT cell capture-writable, so debug readback
  and time travel both go through the configuration path, with no separate CAPTURE register.
- **Frame ECC and readback CRC** (UG470 ch. 8, FRAME_ECCE2): background readback with a
  per-frame syndrome for SEU detection and correction. HANDOFF candidate 2; for silicon it
  should be designed in from the start.
- **MFWR** (UG470 CMD 2): one frame written to many addresses. With §1.4's sparse loads it
  matters less.
- **Authentication.** Any CRC-valid stream loads. For a chip that others will program, an
  HMAC over the stream (and the screening idea in the 2026-09-24 notes) belongs in the
  controller.

## 3. CAD and verification

- **Delays into VPR** (§1.2), then **timing-driven placement and routing in bob's own PnR**
  (Marquardt, Betz & Rose, FPGA 2000; PathFinder's delay term, McMurchie & Ebeling 1995):
  `route.py` and `place.py` are congestion and wirelength only. HANDOFF candidate 5.
- **Seeds and benchmarks.** Architecture decisions rest on 12 small examples and one seed per
  point, and one design (fir16) sets W. The Betz/Rose method uses a benchmark suite and
  geometric means: route MCNC/VTR benchmarks on *virtual* grids (`gridsweep.py` already
  builds graphs that need not fit the board), with several seeds, and keep fir16 and SERV as
  the board-sized proofs.
- **Formal, per bitstream.** Today a build is proven by 300 random cycles plus co-simulation.
  The fabric with its configuration tied to constants is an ordinary netlist: generate a
  formal view of `bob_fabric.v` with `lutk` in place of the CFGLUT5s, constant-propagate the
  word, and prove it sequentially equivalent to the golden netlist with yosys `equiv_*` /
  `eqy`, flip-flops matched through `capture_map`. For the controller, SymbiYosys properties
  (no frame write unless `fdri_ok`; START only after a CRC match after the last FDRI; LFRM
  releases only after `crc_ok`; the TAP state machine) turn today's mutants into proofs.
  Aegis ships a `formal-ip` test for the same reason.
- **Synthesis.** `abc -lut 6` treats every LUT size alike, while bob's element holds two
  5-input functions sharing inputs; a LUT library (`abc -luts`, as `synth_xilinx` does) and
  `abc9` for carry-aware, delay-aware mapping. MIG mapping onto Double Duty chains (Kim &
  Anderson, FPL 2021) is noted in PLAN §M24.

## 4. Toward silicon: what carries over, what has to change

Everything above the switch level carries over: `device.py`, the tileable rr graph, VPR and
bob's PnR, FASM, the frame protocol, the models and the tests. The things that were shaped
by the XC7Z020 have to be redone. In order of how much they decide the chip:

1. **The configuration cell.** On the host, 22,066 of the 68,096 bits are flip-flops and
   37,600 are CFGLUT5 contents. Synthesised as written, every bit becomes a standard-cell
   flip-flop (~20 transistors, about 1.4 M for the device); a latch is about half that, a
   6T SRAM cell 6 (about 0.4 M). FABulous (Koch et al., FPGA 2021; WOSET 2021) builds its
   configuration from frames because "frame-based reconfiguration allows storing
   configuration data in cheap SRAM cells or latches", where a scan chain needs "more
   expensive D-flip-flops". The resource notes' stage 9 says the same: this is the biggest
   area lever.
2. **The frame geometry.** bob's frames are tile-local: 128 bits of one tile, tiles padded to
   whole frames (5 frames per CLB tile, 12% of the bitstream is padding). In 7-series a frame
   spans a column of tiles one clock region high (prjxray's configuration notes), and in
   FABulous "a frame is a column of latches": frame-data lines run along each row
   (FRAME_BITS_PER_ROW) and a frame strobe per frame runs down each column
   (MAX_FRAMES_PER_COL), so one frame writes a few bits in every tile of the column. That
   is what makes the configuration wiring two regular sets of lines in the layout. Decide the
   silicon frame geometry before the tile layouts; FAR, the parser, the CRC and the host
   tools do not change.
3. **Tiles, not a flat netlist.** `fabric_gen.py` emits one flat module holding all 5,695
   routing muxes, with the 100 CLBs (and their 2,400 crossbar muxes) as instances inside it. Grouped by the routing each tile's multiplexers carry (local tracks in, local
   tracks out), bob's 164 routing tiles need **47 distinct layouts**: 23 CLB variants, 10 I/O,
   2 BRAM, 2 DSP and 10 for the cells beside the tall blocks; the commonest covers 34 tiles.
   Most of the variety comes from the grid edges and from the BRAM and DSP columns.
   OpenFPGA's `build_fabric --group_tile` reaches 9 unique tiles for a homogeneous array.
   Generate per-tile modules keyed by that signature (the computation is in
   `docs/learn/layers/gen.py`, `silicon()`), then harden each once and abut them.
4. **Host primitives out.** CFGLUT5 (LUTs and crossbar), the LUT6/MUXF7/MUXF8 routing muxes,
   BUFG, and the RAMB18/DSP48 inference templates are all XC7Z020-specific. `lutk.sv` (M4)
   and the flip-flop crossbar of M21 are still the generic versions; a `--target asic`
   switch in `fabric_gen.py` can emit them with configuration cells. BRAM becomes an
   OpenRAM macro (or DFFRAM), the DSP a synthesised multiplier; Aegis's 128 × 8 BRAM and
   18 × 18 DSP tiles are a size reference for GF180/Sky130.
5. **Muxes as circuits.** Standard-cell mux trees first; pass-transistor trees with
   level-restoring buffers later (Chow et al., TVLSI 1999; Lemieux & Lewis, FPGA 2002), sized
   with COFFE (Chiasson & Betz, FPT 2013; COFFE 2, TRETS 2019).
6. **Timing characterisation.** The loop bob already has (Vivado → `delays.json` → VPR and
   the contract) becomes OpenSTA on each hardened tile → the same `delays.json`. OpenSTA's
   `set_case_analysis` over the configuration bits can also sign off one configured design,
   which Vivado could not do for the emulated fabric.
7. **DFT and bring-up.** Keep CHAIN_IN/CHAIN_OUT: a scan path through every configuration
   cell is the manufacturing test (write patterns, read back). Scan-insert the controller.
   The boundary register belongs in the I/O tiles.
8. **Start small.** One hardened tile on a TinyTapeout slot, then a small array; SOFA
   (LNIS, Sky130) and FABulous are the closest open examples, and Aegis has a full
   Yosys → OpenROAD → KLayout flow for GF180MCU and Sky130 in its `pkgs/aegis-tapeout`.

## 6. Suggested order

**Now (software and docs, the board untouched):** §1.2 delays into the VPR arch (check the
fabric is byte-identical); §1.3 per-mux delays from `write_sdf`; §1.4 the TAP queue and
sparse loads; §1.5 the mode-0 guard; §1.6 the studio token; §1.7 the Makefile banner; the
multi-seed, benchmark-based sweep (§3).

**The next hardware milestone (its own worktree):** absolute Fc (§1.1) together with
whatever it is bundled with (the ARM configuration port of the 2026-09-24 notes is the
obvious partner); two CE/SR per CLB; an async-capable reset; separate INIT and SRVAL.

**Silicon:** decide the configuration cell and the frame geometry first (§4.1–4.2), then
per-tile generation and the asic target (§4.3–4.4), then one tile through OpenLane.
